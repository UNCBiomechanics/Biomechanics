Derivative Lab

Paste raw position data and work out velocity and acceleration by hand first. Then use this page to check your work, see how noise gets amplified with each derivative, and explore where in the chain filtering actually helps. Runs entirely in your browser — nothing is uploaded anywhere.

Two columns = time and position (sampling interval is worked out automatically). One column = position only (set the sampling rate below).

Velocity (1st derivative)

Central difference: v[i] = (x[i+1] − x[i−1]) / (2·Δt) — the same method you'd use by hand, with a simple forward/backward difference at the two endpoints.

Copy data out

Includes a header row — time, position, velocity, acceleration, plus filtered columns once a filter is applied — so it pastes straight into Excel as a labeled table.

Notes: velocity and acceleration are computed with a central difference (forward/backward difference at the two endpoints) — the standard by-hand method, so the numbers here should match your own calculations. Differentiating amplifies high-frequency content, so position data that looks perfectly smooth can produce a visibly noisy acceleration trace — that's not a bug, it's the whole reason filtering matters for kinematic data. The reveal buttons exist so you can work out velocity and acceleration yourself before checking this page.
