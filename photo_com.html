Photo Digitizer

Upload or paste a 2D photo of a person, click to mark joint centers, and this page calculates and draws the whole-body center of mass directly on the image — using the same de Leva (1996) segment parameters as the Anthropometrics Lab. Everything happens in your browser; the photo is never uploaded anywhere.

2. Mark joint centers

Click a landmark below to select it, then click its location on the photo. Click a placed landmark's button again and re-click the photo to move it. Bilateral landmarks are independent — if only one side is visible, just leave the other blank; that side's mass will be doubled and assumed symmetric, same as the Anthropometrics Lab.

3. Center of mass

Segment mass and center-of-mass coefficients: de Leva, P. (1996), "Adjustments to Zatsiorsky-Seluyanov's segment inertia parameters," Journal of Biomechanics 29(9), 1223–1230. The COM shown is computed entirely in image pixel coordinates — there's no calibration to real-world units, since none was requested; the point is to see where COM falls relative to the body, not its absolute position. The hand is approximated at the wrist point (its true mass fraction is under 0.7%, so this has minimal effect); the foot uses the real ankle-to-toe segment. Foot's proximal landmark in de Leva's table is technically the heel rather than the ankle, but the two are close together and the foot's mass fraction is small (~1.4% per side), so this substitution introduces negligible error. If a landmark is missing for one side of a bilateral segment, that segment is excluded from the average unless the other side is available, in which case its mass is doubled at the visible side's own pixel location — correct for a side-on/profile photo (where left and right are superimposed anyway), but it will pull the calculated COM slightly toward whichever side you digitized in a front-facing photo where the other side is genuinely occluded, not just symmetric. The "% of body mass included" stat tells you how complete your digitizing is either way.
