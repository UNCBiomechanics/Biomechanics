KIN 619 — Advanced Biomechanics

Signal Processing & Anthropometrics Tools

Five browser-based tools for working with motion and force data — no installs, no accounts, nothing you paste is ever uploaded anywhere. Pick a tool below.

Suggested order if this is your first pass: Derivative Lab (differentiate position by hand, then check your work) → FFT Quick-Look (see what frequency content is actually in your signal) → Filter Lab (apply a single filter and get clean data back) → Filter Compare (stack multiple filters/datasets to see how choices like order and sample rate change the result) → Anthropometrics Lab (segment-based center of mass, compared against the reaction board method).
01

Derivative Lab

Paste raw position data, work out velocity and acceleration by hand, then reveal the computed derivatives to check your work — and watch how noise gets amplified at each derivative.

Open Derivative Lab
02

FFT Quick-Look

Paste a signal and see its frequency content immediately — time domain on one side, amplitude spectrum on the other.

Open FFT Quick-Look
03

Filter Lab

Apply one Butterworth low-pass/high-pass (1st–4th order) or notch filter, see the before/after, and copy the filtered column back to Excel.

Open Filter Lab
04

Filter Compare

Stack multiple filters on one chart to compare order and time-shift, overlay two datasets at different sampling rates, run a residual analysis to choose a defensible cutoff, and check RMS and half-power points.

Open Filter Compare
05

Anthropometrics Lab

Enter your body segment measurements and calculate whole-body center of mass using de Leva's (1996) parameters, with a to-scale diagram — compare against a reaction board measurement if you have one.

Open Anthropometrics Lab
