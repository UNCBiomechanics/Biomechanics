SES 619 — Ground Reaction Force

GRF Lab

Paste ground reaction force data (time plus one or more force components), filter it, and click two points directly on the chart to measure rate of force development or impulse. Runs entirely in your browser — nothing is uploaded anywhere.

1. Your data

If your export (e.g. from Vicon) has several header rows (file path, channel name, units, axis), delete all but the one row with the column names you want to use, so the pasted header is a single row.

2. Columns & filter

Tell us what each pasted column actually is — nothing is assumed from column order or position.

Assign whichever of your remaining columns are the force components you want to work with. Leave any axis set to "None" if you don't have that component (e.g. a vertical-only load cell).

GRF signals carry real energy at high frequencies (impact peaks, rapid loading) — an aggressive cutoff will blunt exactly the peak magnitude and rate of force development you're trying to measure. If you're not sure what cutoff is defensible for your task, use Filter Compare first.

Force vs. time

Scroll or pinch to zoom, drag to pan. While a pick mode below is armed, clicking the chart records a point instead of panning.

Point-to-point analysis

Rate of force development (RFD) between two picked points A and B is the average slope ΔF⁄Δt = (FB−FA)⁄(tB−tA) — not the instantaneous (peak) RFD, which would need a derivative over a moving window (see Derivative Lab for that). Impulse is the trapezoidal-rule integral of the filtered force curve between the two picked times, ∫F dt ≈ Σ ½(Fn+Fn+1)(tn+1−tn), shown as the shaded region under the curve. Both use whichever component is selected as "Component to analyze," and both read from the filtered curve (or the raw curve if filtering is turned off). Low-pass filtering is the same fixed 1st-order Butterworth pass-cascade used in Filter Lab — order sets how many alternating-direction passes are cascaded together; even orders end zero-lag, odd orders keep one pass's worth of lag.
