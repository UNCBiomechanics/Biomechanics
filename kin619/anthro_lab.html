Reaction board method (optional)

Classic moment-balance technique: a rigid board rests on a fixed knife-edge at the feet end and a scale at the head end. Tare the scale with the board alone (R0), then have the subject lie fully extended on the board and read the scale again (R1) — once for each arm position you want to compare. Distance from the feet to the subject's COM: d = (R1 − R0) × L / W.

Leave blank to use the body mass entered above. Must be in the same units as R0/R1 (kg or lb — just be consistent).

Leave any R1 field blank to skip that trial — the calculated (de Leva) prediction for that position will still show on its own.

Calculated vs. reaction board, by arm position

Raising the arms shifts mass toward the head, so whole-body COM moves higher (a larger distance from the feet). This lets you check whether your measured shift between positions matches the predicted one.

Segment mass, center-of-mass, and radius-of-gyration coefficients: de Leva, P. (1996), "Adjustments to Zatsiorsky-Seluyanov's segment inertia parameters," Journal of Biomechanics 29(9), 1223–1230. Simplifications used here: feet are assumed to lie flat on the ground, so foot mass is included at ground level rather than positioned along the (horizontal) heel-to-toe axis; the small vertical thickness of the foot at the ankle is neglected; left and right sides are assumed symmetric from a single-side measurement; the head segment's C7 landmark is approximated at shoulder height. These are standard, small simplifications for a teaching-lab estimate — they are also exactly the kind of assumptions worth comparing against your reaction-board measurement to see how much they matter in practice. The moment-of-inertia section adds one more assumption: each limb segment's own long axis is treated as aligned with the body's vertical axis, which holds for the arms-at-sides posture modeled there but not for a limb swung out to the side.
