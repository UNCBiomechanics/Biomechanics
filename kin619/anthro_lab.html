Anthropometrics Lab

Measure your own body segment lengths, and this page calculates your standing whole-body center of mass using de Leva's (1996) adjusted Zatsiorsky-Seluyanov segment parameters — the standard reference used throughout biomechanics. If your lab section also used the reaction board technique to measure COM directly, enter that value too and compare it against the calculated one.

Segment lengths

Measure one side only (left = right is assumed) with a tape measure or anthropometer, in centimeters. Assumes standard anatomical standing position, arms hanging straight down at the sides.

Base of neck (C7) to top of head (vertex)

Base of neck / mid-shoulder to mid-point between hips

Shoulder (acromion) to elbow (lateral epicondyle)

Elbow to wrist (styloid process)

Wrist to tip of middle finger

Hip (greater trochanter) to knee joint line

Knee joint line to ankle (lateral malleolus)

Reaction board method (optional)

Classic moment-balance technique: a rigid board rests on a fixed knife-edge at the feet end and a scale at the head end. Tare the scale with the board alone (R0), then have the subject lie fully extended on the board and read the scale again (R1) — once for each arm position you want to compare. Distance from the feet to the subject's COM: d = (R1 − R0) × L / W.

Leave blank to use the body mass entered above. Must be in the same units as R0/R1 (kg or lb — just be consistent).

Leave any R1 field blank to skip that trial — the calculated (de Leva) prediction for that position will still show on its own.

Calculated vs. reaction board, by arm position

Raising the arms shifts mass toward the head, so whole-body COM moves higher (a larger distance from the feet). This lets you check whether your measured shift between positions matches the predicted one.

Body diagram

Body outline drawn to scale from your segment lengths (arms-at-sides skeleton shown for reference; the diagram doesn't re-draw the arm angle per position). Solid lines = calculated COM. Dashed lines = reaction board COM. Color marks the arm position.

Segment mass and center-of-mass coefficients: de Leva, P. (1996), "Adjustments to Zatsiorsky-Seluyanov's segment inertia parameters," Journal of Biomechanics 29(9), 1223–1230. Simplifications used here: feet are assumed to lie flat on the ground, so foot mass is included at ground level rather than positioned along the (horizontal) heel-to-toe axis; the small vertical thickness of the foot at the ankle is neglected; left and right sides are assumed symmetric from a single-side measurement; the head segment's C7 landmark is approximated at shoulder height. These are standard, small simplifications for a teaching-lab estimate — they are also exactly the kind of assumptions worth comparing against your reaction-board measurement to see how much they matter in practice.
